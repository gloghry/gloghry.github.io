## Guidance and sources

- [Copyright Office: copyright basics](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-general.html): eligible original work is protected when created and fixed; facts and ideas themselves are not protected. Registration is separate, generally required before suing over a U.S. work, and timely registration can affect available remedies.
- [Copyright Office: notice](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ03.pdf): notice is optional for modern works. A conventional notice identifies the symbol, applicable first-publication year, and copyright owner; it does not establish ownership or replace permissions.
- [Copyright Office: photographs](https://www.copyright.gov/engage/photographers/): the photographer is generally the initial owner, subject to exceptions such as work made for hire. Being the subject or possessing a copy does not itself establish ownership.
- [Copyright Office: AI and copyrightability](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html): assistance from AI does not automatically prevent protection for human-authored expression; do not assume every generated element is protected.
- [USPTO: trademarks and symbols](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark): trademarks identify a source of goods/services. TM/SM and the registration symbol have different uses; registration symbols require applicable registration.
